# Chapter Craft

Load this when prose quality, serial pacing, or an A-style rewrite is the main concern. It supports the three-pass method in `SKILL.md` without imposing one template on every chapter.

## Before Writing

Identify what the viewpoint character wants now, what concrete pressure blocks them, what choice the chapter forces, and who pays. A 3-5-scene plan is a useful starting shape, not a law. Keep a live consequence from the prior chapter in view. The ending should make the next chapter matter, but it must not promise a reveal the next chapter cannot deliver.

## Free Draft

Write scene-first and character-first. Let dialogue arise from opposed aims and differing speech habits. Use professional knowledge through a visible action or decision, not a paragraph of terminology. A sensation matters when it changes what someone does, fears, buys, hides, or misreads.

Local invention is allowed if it does not violate a hard world rule, erase an irreversible loss, or assign future knowledge to an earlier character. A newly invented fact that would constrain later chapters remains a candidate.

## Canon Lock

Compare the draft with the compact brief and exact source spans. Check material details, event order, injuries, resources, status, objects, and knowledge sources. Repair real contradictions in the scene rather than adding explanatory narration to pass an audit. If a divergence can be reconciled in the current or next two chapters, record its intended resolution and expiry backstage.

## Human Voice

Replace abstract judgments with decisions, gestures, or consequences when that increases clarity. Keep sentence lengths varied and dialogue character-specific. Cut repeated explanation, universal-sounding maxims, and symmetrical “not X but Y” constructions when they flatten the scene. Do not remove a necessary fact merely to make prose shorter.

Aim for an opening with pressure and a forward ending, but do not force a fixed percentage or a cliffhanger in every chapter. Quiet aftermath can be a strong ending when it changes the relationship or the reader's question. No metric or audit label belongs in the finished prose.
